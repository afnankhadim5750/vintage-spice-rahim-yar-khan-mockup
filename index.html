01 — The Board

What the kitchen
is known for.

Eight dishes, two fires. The karahi side cooks low and slow; the grill side cooks over coals. Everything below is on the menu every day the restaurant is open.

White Chicken Karahi Signature

Creamy, green-chilli karahi — the dish the city names first when it names Vintage Spice.

Chicken Karahi

The classic desi karahi — tomatoes, ginger, green chillies, cooked in the wok it is served in.

Green Chilli Handi

Handi-cooked chicken with a proper green-chilli bite, finished with fresh coriander.

Daal Makhni

Slow-simmered black lentils with butter and cream — the quiet favourite next to the karahi.

Seekh Kabab

Hand-pressed, charcoal-grilled, served straight off the skewer.

Gola Kabab

Soft, smoky gola kabab from the grill — a BBQ-spread regular.

Turkish Boti

Char-grilled boti from the Middle Eastern side of the menu.

Green Boti

Green-marinated boti, grilled over coals and served with naan.

02 — The White Karahi

One karahi,
one reputation.

Ask around Rahim Yar Khan for the best white chicken karahi and this is the name that comes back. It is cooked the slow way — chicken simmered in a creamy, green-chilli gravy until it comes off the bone — and it arrives at the table still bubbling in the karahi it was cooked in.

  1. 1

    In the karahi

    Chicken goes into the wok with ginger, green chillies and cream — no shortcuts, no pre-made base.

  2. 2

    Simmered slow

    It cooks down until the gravy thickens and the chicken turns soft enough to pull apart.

  3. 3

    Served bubbling

    Straight from the fire to the table, with fresh naan and green chutney on the side.

BBQ, off the coals.

The grill runs beside the karahi — seekh and gola kabab, Turkish boti and green boti, cooked over charcoal and served with naan. It is the other half of the menu, and the reason the BBQ spread gets talked about as much as the karahi.

04 — Rice, Noodles & the Tandoor

Beyond the karahi.

Desi, Chinese and a little Italian share the menu — white biryani and chicken fried rice for the table, chowmein and penne pasta for the ones who want something different, and naan straight out of the tandoor for all of it.

Chicken Fried Rice

Wok-tossed rice with chicken, peppers and spring onion.

Chowmein

Stir-fried noodles with vegetables, cooked to order in the pan.

White Biryani & Daal

White biryani for the table — and daal makhni with steamed rice, the way regulars order it.

From the tandoor

Naan comes out of the clay oven all day — blistered, buttered, and made to scoop up karahi, handi and daal. Penne pasta is on the menu too, for the table that wants one Italian plate among the desi ones.

10 — Questions

Asked often.

What is Vintage Spice best known for?

Its white chicken karahi — reviewers call it the best white chicken karahi in Rahim Yar Khan. The BBQ spread and the green chilli handi run close behind.

When is the dinner buffet?

Dinner buffets run on announced event days, 19:00–22:30. Hi-tea runs 12:30–16:00 on event days. Dates are posted on the restaurant’s Facebook page — call ahead to confirm.

Can I book the rooftop for a Nikah or birthday?

Yes. The rooftop and event hall host Nikah dinners and birthday gatherings for 65–70 guests. Bookings are taken by phone on 0312 2228510.

Do you deliver?

Yes — delivery is free within Abbasia Town, and free citywide on orders over Rs 2,000. Order by phone.

Is there anything besides desi food?

Yes — the menu runs three ways: desi (karahi, handi, daal, biryani), Middle Eastern and BBQ (Turkish boti, green boti, seekh and gola kabab), and Chinese and Italian (chicken fried rice, chowmein, penne pasta).

What are the opening hours?

General daily opening hours aren’t published. The verified times are lunch from ~12:30, hi-tea 12:30–16:00 and dinner buffet 19:00–22:30 on event days. Call 0312 2228510 before you come.
